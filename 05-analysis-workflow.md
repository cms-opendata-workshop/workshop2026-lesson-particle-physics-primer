## Background

Dozens or even hundreds of particle physics analyses are performed 
every year. It's challenging and not every analysis is the same, but
there is a basic workflow that can be helpful to understand. 

In this lesson, we provide material for a self-guided walkthrough
of the individual steps and overarching workflow of a particle physics
analysis.

The material is presented at a *conceptual* level, without going into 
the details or describing the specific analysis tools. These materials 
were presented at other workshops which might be different than this one, 
so you may see or hear some jargon which goes unexplained. Feel free
to keep notes and ask your facilitators about these terms during
this workshop. 


## CMS DAS (CMS Data Analysis School) - "Analysis Overview"

Every year, the CMS experiment organizes a number of *Data Analysis Schools* that
we refer to as [CMSDAS](https://lpc.fnal.gov/programs/schools-workshops/cmsdas.shtml). 
These serve as an introduction to new undergraduate students,
graduate students,
and post-docs as well as a refresher for any collaborator who wants to know
the latest computational tools and techniques. 

In 2021, Matt Bellis (Siena University) provided an analysis overview at a
very high level, designed to map out different components of an analysis. 
The video and the slides are provided below.

## More on the analysis workflow

In 2024, at the Workshop on High Energy Physics Phenomenology (WHEPP), the CMS
DPOA group ran a remote workshop on how to access and use
our open data. Participants eventually worked to reconstruct the top
quark and measure its properties. 

At the start of that exercise, we gave a conceptual overview
of the steps involved in a general analysis and the tools the participants 
we would use. We do not have a recording of the presentations, but the slides
have enough text that a reader should be able to follow the main
points.
